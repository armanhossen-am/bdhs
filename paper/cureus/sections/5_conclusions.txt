Analyses of the BDHS can reproduce the official estimates almost exactly. Doing so depends on handling the ever-married samples, questionnaire changes, country-specific conventions and survey design correctly; getting them wrong shifts key indicators by up to ten percentage points. Writing these decisions down once, in validated and openly available software, makes BDHS analyses more accurate, quicker to carry out and easier to check. We suggest that analyses of DHS data routinely report how their national estimates compare with the official figures.
